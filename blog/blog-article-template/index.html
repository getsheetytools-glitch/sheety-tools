FIRST PARAGRAPH. Should restate the article's core promise and include the primary keyword naturally. This paragraph sets the hook — it tells the reader why this matters to them specifically.

Second intro paragraph. Establish the cost of the problem or the scale of what's at stake. Reference data if you have it — this is where the BambooHR 44-day stat or Enboarder 90-day departure data lives.

44
The average number of days it takes a new hire to reach full productivity. Most companies don't have a plan for those 44 days. Source: BambooHR Employee Onboarding Research

Section Heading One — Make It Keyword-Rich

Section body copy. Be direct. One idea per paragraph. The voice is punchy and opinionated — back claims with research but don't over-hedge.

What Actually Works

Every article that diagnoses a problem should offer a directional fix. This doesn't have to be a complete guide — it's a bridge to the product without being a pitch.

Frame the solution in a way that's useful whether or not the reader uses Sheety. This is what earns trust. The product mention at the end should feel earned, not inserted.

The Bottom Line

Short closing paragraph. Restate the core insight in one or two sentences. What should the reader take with them?

Light, natural product mention. "If you want a head start, Sheety Onboarding does X." One sentence max. Never the main point of the closing.
